1. What is PunctuAction?

The problem. During role-play, many pupils read dialogue lines in a flat, monotonous “robot” voice. They run through commas and full stops, read questions as if they were statements, and show no feeling at exclamation marks.

The idea. Punctuation is a set of instructions for the voice. PunctuAction turns each mark into a character that tells pupils what to do: Dotty . says STOP, Koma , says BREATHE, Quizzy ? says ASK (Yes/No questions rise ↗, WH questions fall ↘), and Zing ! says FEEL. Pupils help Bip, a robot who reads with no feeling, to earn four voice chips.

The journey. Learn (meet the marks) → Practice (six activities at Easy, Medium and Hard levels with new sentences every round) → Reading Studio (listen to a model, record, see your own voice melody line, role-play) → Dialogue Scanner (bring any dialogue, even from a photo) → Show & grow (badges, teacher feedback) → Feedback. Every part has its own narrated video tutorial.

2. Quick start

🎒 Pupils

  1. Open PunctuAction and tap I'm a pupil.
  2. Tap your name (and your 3 secret pictures if your teacher set them).
  3. Follow the journey on the Home page, or tap Continue for the best next step. Tap 🎬 Watch how on any page for its video tutorial.

🔒 Teachers

  1. Tap I'm a teacher, create a PIN and save your recovery code.
  2. Add pupils, record your own model voice, set the Reading Check to Pre-test.
  3. Teach, then switch to Post-test and open Evaluation.
Offline: open the app once with internet from its web address and choose Install app (or “Add to Home screen”). After that it works with no internet. You can also copy the whole folder to a laptop and open index.html. Everything (fonts, sounds, characters) is inside the app.

3. Pupil guide

For pupils who are still learning to read: every instruction has a 🔊 button that reads it aloud, and Settings can read every instruction automatically. Bahasa Melayu help can also be switched on.

📚 Learn: Meet the Mark Crew

Each lesson has five steps: Meet (the character talks), Trace (draw the mark with your finger), Hear the difference (e.g. “Let's eat Grandma!” vs “Let's eat, Grandma!”), Spot (tap every mark of that kind in a short story) and Say it (record yourself and get tips).

🎭 Reading Studio

  1. Choose a dialogue (12 are included, from Year 1 to Year 6, plus any your teacher writes).
  2. Tap the character you want to be. Turn the voice signs on (🛑 💨 ↗ ↘ 😄 above the marks) or off for a challenge.
  3. Press 🔊 Listen to hear the model (your teacher's voice if recorded, otherwise the computer voice).
  4. Press 🎙 Record, read, then ⏹ Stop. You see your voice melody line, your pauses, the Robot-o-meter and tips.
  5. Tick the self-check and press 📨 Send to my teacher.
  6. Try 🎬 Role-play mode: the app reads the other characters and records you when it is your turn, then plays the whole scene back.
🎭 Voice disguise (for shy pupils). Choose 🐭 Little Mouse, 🐻 Big Bear or 👽 Space Alien. The app shifts the pitch of your recording but keeps every pause, rise and fall in the same place, so your teacher can still assess your punctuation reading without hearing your real voice.

4. Dialogue Scanner (bring your own dialogue)

Pupils can bring any dialogue: from the textbook, a storybook, or one they wrote. They can type it, paste it, take a photo or upload a picture. Photo reading uses an OCR engine built into the app, so it works offline and the picture never leaves the device.

  1. Write each line as Name: words (lines with “quotation marks… said Ali” also work). A heading such as “Unit 5: At the Zoo” becomes the title.
  2. Press Scan. Bip counts every mark, gives a voice-ready score and lists punctuation problems: missing end marks, questions that end with a full stop, missing commas after Yes / Oh / Well or names, capital letters. Each comes with a reason and a one-tap Fix (or Fix all).
  3. The voice map shows what the voice does at every mark (🛑 stop, 💨 breathe, ↗ up for Yes/No, ↘ strong start and down for WH, 💥 feeling). Tap any mark for an explanation. Bip guesses the feeling for each “!”, and pupils can change it.
  4. Press Listen to hear it with a different voice for each speaker, then Practise it in the Studio: it becomes the pupil's own role-play, with recording and Role-play mode.

5. Video tutorials

Every part of the app has a narrated, animated video. Press 🎬 Watch how on any page, or open the Help Centre. The videos show the real app on a tablet, with a moving pointer, zoom and highlights, a character presenter and word-by-word subtitles.

The voice and the animation always match. Each part waits until its sentence has been spoken before moving on, and each highlight starts with its own sentence. Controls: play/pause, previous/next part, replay, speed (0.8× / 1× / 1.2×), subtitles, narration on/off and full screen. Without a computer voice, the subtitles are shown for their reading time.

7. Teacher guide

🎙️ My model voices (strongly recommended)

Record yourself reading each dialogue line once. Pupils then hear your voice as the model everywhere, and your voice line appears as a grey dashed guide on their melody line. You can also pick which computer voice each character uses.

🎧 Assess recordings

Listen, look at the melody line and Bip's analysis (pauses heard, ending tone, pitch range, Robot-o-meter), read the pupil's self-check, then score the 5-criterion rubric (Full stop, Comma, Question, Exclamation, Expression & fluency; 4 bands each). The Hub suggests a PBD level (TP1–6); your judgement is final. Add stickers and a comment; the pupil sees them in their mailbox.

📈 Evaluation & feedback

Evidence for the Testing stage: pre-test vs post-test rubric averages (class and each pupil), Smileyometer and Again-Again results (Fun Toolkit, Read & MacFarlane), what pupils found tricky, favourite characters, usage per game and pupils' ideas. Export everything as CSV.

🗂️ Pupils on different devices

Pupil device: Me → Save my work as a file. Teacher device: Data & settings → Import. To send your pupil list, dialogues and model voices to pupil devices: Export class pack, then on each pupil device Settings → Load class pack. Files can travel by USB, Bluetooth, Google Classroom or DELIMa.

Demo class: Data & settings → Load demo class fills the Hub with 8 sample pupils, practice history, feedback and synthetic “hum” recordings (flat pre-test, expressive post-test). Use it to explore or present, then Remove demo data.

8. How the computer voice follows punctuation

The browser's built-in voices read text in one go, so they often rush through punctuation. PunctuAction's voice engine splits every line at its punctuation and controls each part:

MarkWhat the engine does
, commaSpeaks the clause with a “continue” tone, then adds a short breath pause (about 0.4 s; 0.65 s in Teaching mode).
. full stopFalling tone, then a full stop pause (about 0.8 s; 1.25 s in Teaching mode).
? Yes/NoKeeps the “?” and lifts the pitch so the voice rises ↗ at the end.
? WHDetects the WH word, starts slightly higher and speaks the ending as a statement so the voice falls ↘, which is the usual English intonation.
! exclamationUses the line's feeling (happy, excited, angry, surprised, scared, sad, worried, warning, proud) to change pitch, speed and loudness.
Computer voices differ between devices and are never as natural as a person. For the most accurate model, record your own voice in the Teacher Hub. Your recording is always used first. On devices without an English voice, the app still animates the timing and asks the teacher to record.

9. Privacy, safety and ethics

  • Photos in the Dialogue Scanner are read on the device by the built-in OCR engine. They are never uploaded or stored.
  • No accounts, no internet, no tracking. Names, recordings and results are stored only in this device's browser (IndexedDB).
  • The Teacher Hub needs a PIN (stored as a salted hash), blocks guessing after 5 wrong tries, auto-locks when idle, and is not linked from the pupil app.
  • Ask Bip is rule-based and runs offline, so pupils' words and voices are never sent to a public AI tool.
  • Voice disguise protects shy pupils while keeping their reading assessable.
  • Use first names or nicknames, and get school and guardian permission before trials.
  • All characters and artwork are original and drawn in code. Fonts are Fredoka and Andika (SIL Open Font License). Sound effects, the jingle and the music are generated in the app. Photo reading uses Tesseract OCR (Apache License 2.0). The natural character voices (dialogues, feelings sentences) were generated with ElevenLabs and are bundled in the app, so they also work offline; lines without a recording use the device voice, and the teacher's own recording always comes first.

10. Troubleshooting

The Scanner cannot read my photoUse a bright, straight, close photo of printed text. Always check the words before pressing Scan. Photo reading needs the app opened from its web address or installed (not opened as a file).
No sound from the computer voiceCheck the volume. In Settings choose another voice. Install an English text-to-speech voice on the device, or record the teacher's voice.
The microphone does not workAllow microphone access when the browser asks. Use Chrome or Edge on Android, Windows or Chromebook, or Safari on iPad. Recording needs the app opened from its web address (https) or as an installed app.
Forgot the teacher PINOn the lock screen tap Forgot PIN? and enter the recovery code.
Pupils' work is on another tabletUse “Save my work as a file”, then import it in Data & settings.
Bip's tips seem wrongBip's analysis is a helpful guide, not a final mark. Noise can confuse it. The teacher's rubric score is the real assessment.
